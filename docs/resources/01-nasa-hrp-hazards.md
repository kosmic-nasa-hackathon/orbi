# NASA HRP — 5 Hazards of Human Spaceflight

- **Source:** https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/hazards/ (plus one subpage per hazard, linked in each section)
- **Organization:** NASA · Human Research Program (HRP)
- **Consulted:** 13 Nov 2026

## Summary

The HRP groups the challenges of crewed spaceflight into **five hazards**: space radiation, isolation and confinement, distance from Earth, gravity fields, and hostile/closed environments. The page stresses that they **do not act separately**: *"They can feed off one another and exacerbate effects on the human body"*. They are studied in terrestrial analogs, in laboratories and on the ISS.

## Quick summary

| Hazard | Key idea | Citable fact | ORBI system |

## 1. Space Radiation

**Subpage:** https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/hazard-space-radiation/

- *"Space radiation is invisible to the human eye. It's not only stealthy, but considered one of the most hazardous aspects of human spaceflight."*
- Outside Earth's magnetic field and atmosphere, **ionizing radiation** is a serious risk on the journey to Mars. It has two origins:
  - **Galactic cosmic rays** of high energy (supernova remnants)
  - **Solar storms**: *solar particle events* (SPE) and coronal mass ejections
- Astronauts **cannot see or feel** radiation. NASA is developing ways to monitor it and protect against this *"silent hazard"*.
- **Effects:** *"Radiation exposure increases cancer risk, damages the central nervous system, can alter cognitive function, reduce motor function, and prompt behavioral changes."*
- NASA studies the effects on biological samples at the **NASA Space Radiation Laboratory (NSRL)**, on the ground.
- NASA follows **specific procedures to continuously monitor** radiation and each astronaut's exposure.

## 2. Isolation and Confinement

**Subpage:** https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/hazard-isolation-and-confinement/

- Prolonged isolation and confinement bring out behavioral responses. Crews are **selected, trained and supported** to work as a team for months or years.
- **The key sentence:** *"Sleep loss, circadian desynchronization, and work overload compound this issue and may lead to decreased performance, adverse health outcomes, and compromised mission objectives."*
- **What NASA does:** *"experts are developing methods for monitoring behavioral health and adapting/refining various tools and technologies for use in the spaceflight environment to detect and treat early risk factors."*
- Research lines: **workload and performance**, **light therapy to align the circadian rhythm**, phase shift and alertness.
- **Personal journaling** is a tool used by behavioral scientists to assess mental and emotional state.
- **Ground analogs:**
  - **HERA**: 650 ft² two-story habitat; volunteer crews for **45 days**.
  - **CHAPEA**: simulates **one-year** stays on Mars; **4 crew members** in Mars Dune Alpha (1,700 ft²).

## 3. Distance from Earth

**Subpage:** https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/hazard-distance-from-earth/

- Instructions, supplies and **medical care** become increasingly hard to receive from Earth.
- Mars is on average **140 million miles** away; the journey would take **~3 years** (versus 3 days to the Moon).
- **The key sentence:** *"Facing a communication delay of **up to 20 minutes one way**, the possibility of equipment failures or **medical emergencies**, and a critical need to ration food and supplies, astronauts must be capable of confronting an array of situations with **minimal support from teams on Earth**."*
- HERA already practices **delays of up to 5 minutes one way** to design deep-space procedures.
- **Three-step strategy toward Mars:** (1) Earth simulations → (2) ISS research → (3) Artemis lunar missions.
- Food: systems with minimal resources and waste; **"menu fatigue"** (getting tired of eating the same thing) can have serious consequences on long trips.

## 4. Gravity Fields

**Subpage:** https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/hazard-gravity-fields/

- Weightlessness can cause **dizziness, bone and muscle loss, vision problems** and more. After landing on the Moon or Mars, astronauts may need time to adapt before critical tasks.
- **Three gravities in a Mars mission:**
  1. **~6 months** of weightlessness on the way
  2. **~1/3 of Earth gravity** on Mars (the main page says 3/8)
  3. Readaptation to Earth gravity on return
- **The transitions affect** spatial orientation, head-eye and hand-eye coordination, balance and locomotion; some suffer **space motion sickness**.
- Going from weightlessness to gravity: possible **lightheadedness and fainting**.
- **Bone:** *"weight-bearing bones lose on average **1% to 1.5% of mineral density per month** during spaceflight"* (linked source: [HRP Evidence Report – Fracture](https://humanresearchroadmap.nasa.gov/Evidence/reports/Fracture.pdf)).
- **Fluids:** they shift toward the head, which can press on the eyes and cause **vision problems** (related to SANS).
- **Kidney:** without preventive measures, higher risk of **kidney stones** from **dehydration** and from calcium leaving the bones.
- **Research platforms:** :envihab (head-down tilt bed rest, DLR), Neutral Buoyancy Laboratory (6.2-million-gallon pool), ARGOS (a crane that simulates partial gravity) and the ISS.

**Further reading:**
- [HRP Evidence Report – Fracture (PDF)](https://humanresearchroadmap.nasa.gov/Evidence/reports/Fracture.pdf): **source with numerical bone data**
- [New Tests Evaluate Mission Readiness of Astronauts Upon Landing](https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/new-tests-evaluate-mission-readiness-of-astronauts-upon-landing/)
- [NASA Fluid Shifts Study](https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/nasa-fluid-shifts-study-advances-journey-to-mars/)

- [Podcast: Fighting space effects](https://www.nasa.gov/podcasts/houston-we-have-a-podcast/fighting-space-effects/)

**In ORBI:**
- **1–1.5% bone density per month** is the star fact for Musculoskeletal's "Why this matters": *"Without gravity, weight-bearing bones lose 1–1.5% of their mineral density every month. Exercise is your countermeasure."* With an official source.
- **The fact can be used in the demo:** on day 148 (~5 months) an estimated loss of ~5–7.5% **without countermeasures**. Exercise adherence makes sense. (It is a linear estimate and must be presented as such.)
- **Lightheadedness and fainting during transitions** support the roadmap's orthostatic correlation and the orthostatic test (Level 2).
- **Kidney stones from dehydration** open a hydration countermeasure for Metabolic (action card: *"Increase fluid intake"*).
- **Fluids toward the head → vision** is the argument for the Amsler test (Level 2).
- **The three gravities** are the basis of the roadmap's "mission phase" concept (thresholds change in transit, on the surface and on return).

## 5. Hostile/Closed Environments

**Subpage:** https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/hazard-hostile-closed-environments/

- Keeping a safe ecosystem inside the vehicle requires controlling temperature, pressure, lighting, noise, **microbial communities** and **immune response**.
- **The key sentence:** *"Microbes can change characteristics in space, and micro-organisms that naturally live on the human body are **transferred more easily from person to person in closed habitats**... Stress hormone levels can elevate and the immune system can alter, which could lead to **increased susceptibility to allergies or other illnesses**."* NASA adds that more research is needed to know whether these are serious risks.
- Habitability: nobody wants to live and work in a place that is too hot or cold, cramped, crowded, noisy or poorly lit.
- At HERA, crew members take swab samples to analyze microbes and **check the status of the simulated environment** (*"Breathing Easy"*).

**Further reading:**
- [Scientists Probe How Long-Term Spaceflight Alters Immunity](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/scientists-probe-how-long-term-spaceflight-alters-immunity/)
- [Podcast: Hazard 5 – Environments](https://www.nasa.gov/podcasts/houston-we-have-a-podcast/hazard-5-environments/) (a NASA immunologist)
- [Breathing Easy: HERA Crew Member Checks Status of Mock Environment](https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/breathing-easy-hera-crew-member-checks-status-of-mock-environment/)
- [HERA Crew Member Stores Swabs for Analyzing Microbes](https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/hera-crew-member-stores-swabs-for-analyzing-microbes/)

**In ORBI:**
- **Easier transmission between people in closed habitats** justifies a concrete action in the `early-infection` correlation: *"Mask in common areas, rest in crew quarters"* (already in the action card). And in the CMO view (Level 2), alerting the rest of the crew.
- **Stress → immune changes → infections**: it is the official chain linking Behavioral and Immune. It supports the sleep + temperature correlation.
- **Environment and body are inseparable** according to NASA: it reinforces the cabin badge and **"Cabin issue, not you"**.
- Noise, light and habitat temperature are possible environment badges (roadmap).
