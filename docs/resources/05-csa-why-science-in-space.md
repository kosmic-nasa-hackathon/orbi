# CSA — Why do we conduct science experiments in space?

- **Source:** https://www.asc-csa.gc.ca/eng/iss/science/why-do-we-conduct-science-experiments-in-space.asp
- **Organization:** Canadian Space Agency (CSA)
- **Page last modified:** 28 Oct 2025 · **Consulted:** 13 Nov 2026

## Summary

CSA explains why it conducts science on the ISS, with two objectives: **preparing deep-space missions** and **improving quality of life on Earth** with what is learned.

## Preparing for deep space

The objective is to understand the risks of crewed flight and find **countermeasures and treatments** to *identify, characterize and mitigate* the effects on health. Missions to the Moon and Mars involve longer journeys and more risks:

- more time in weightlessness
- more exposure to radiation
- longer periods of isolation, very far from home
- **more delay in communications with Earth**
- **less ability to depend on Earth** for food, materials and **emergency medical assistance**

## Benefits on Earth

- The effects of weightlessness resemble **accelerated aging** and the problems of a **sedentary life**.
- Studying the body for six months in space yields data that on Earth would **take years** to collect.
- Canadian scientists study **bones, heart, blood vessels and brain**. Their results help with balance problems (older people), osteoporosis, cardiovascular disorders and **type 2 diabetes**.
- On average there are **~200 international experiments** aboard the ISS at any given time.
